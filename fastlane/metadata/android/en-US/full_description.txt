Most weather apps lead with a temperature and a picture of a sun. The question people actually open one to answer is narrower and more urgent:

Is it going to rain, when, how hard, and when will it stop?

Wetter is built around that question. Precipitation gets the emphasis, the colour and the space. Temperature is deliberately quieter.

<b>A precision instrument, not a dashboard</b>

No cartoon weather, no stacks of cards, no gradients used as decoration. Restrained typography, hairline rules, and a palette where rain owns the only saturated colour — so a glance tells you what is coming without anything competing for your attention. It looks right in both light and dark.

<b>It picks the best source for where you are</b>

Wetter knows about several national and international meteorological services, what each is good at and where. It prefers a regional model where one runs a fine grid, falls back to a global one elsewhere, and quietly switches when a service is unavailable. You never have to know which one answered.

Where the regional model has the finer forecast but the shorter reach, Wetter uses it for the near term and continues with a longer-range source afterwards, so there is always an hour-by-hour timeline.

<b>It works without a connection</b>

The forecast you already have appears immediately. A failed update leaves it on screen and tells you how old it is, rather than replacing it with an error.

<b>Privacy is the architecture, not a promise</b>

• No account
• No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking of any kind
• One permission: internet access
• Location access is optional — the app is fully usable without it
• No server of ours. Your device asks a weather service directly, and nothing sits in between

The only thing that leaves your device is a pair of coordinates, sent to a weather service so it can answer. That is unavoidable for a weather app. Making it the only thing is the point.
