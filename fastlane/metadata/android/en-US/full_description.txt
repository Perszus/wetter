Most weather apps lead with a temperature and a picture of a sun. The question people actually open one to answer is narrower and more urgent:

Is it going to rain, when, how heavy, and when will it stop?

Wetter is built around that question. Precipitation gets the emphasis, the colour and the space, and temperature stays quieter.

<b>Rain, down to the minute</b>

For the next two hours the forecast comes from the rain radar: where the rain is now and where it is moving. After that it hands over to the weather models. A rain map plays the last hour of radar and the next two hours ahead.

<b>The right source for where you are</b>

Wetter knows several meteorological services and what each is good at. It prefers a regional model where one runs a fine grid, uses a global one elsewhere, and switches when a service is unavailable. Where the regional model has the finer forecast but the shorter reach, a longer-range source continues it, so the timeline stays hour by hour.

<b>Widgets</b>

There are five home-screen widgets, each answering one question: the next four hours of rain, the weather now, the coming hours, the week, and an overview of all of them. Each lays itself out for the size it is given.

<b>Warnings worth having</b>

Wetter notifies you of gales, torrential rain, heavy snow, ice, thunderstorms and dangerous temperatures, judged against what is normal for your location. Nothing else interrupts you, and warnings can be switched off.

<b>A precision instrument, not a dashboard</b>

There is no cartoon weather and no stack of cards, only restrained type, hairline rules and a palette in which rain owns the only saturated colour. It has light and dark themes, and Celsius or Fahrenheit, millimetres or inches, as you prefer.

<b>It works without a connection</b>

The forecast you already have appears immediately. A failed update leaves it on screen and tells you how old it is.

<b>Privacy</b>

• No account, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking
• No server of ours: your device asks the weather services directly
• Location access is optional, and used once, when you ask for your current location
• What leaves your device is the location's coordinates, sent to the services that answer, and on the maps the tiles around it

Wetter asks for internet and network access, for location and notifications (both optional), and to start after a reboot so the background refresh carries on.
